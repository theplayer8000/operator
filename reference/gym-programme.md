## The one thing that matters most

You have two years of detraining and two years of prior training. Those pull in
opposite directions: your nervous system remembers how to lift heavy, but your
tendons, ligaments and connective tissue have **not** kept pace with what your
muscles and technique will let you attempt.

This is the single most common way returning lifters get hurt — the technique
is still there, the confidence is still there, the load tolerance isn't. Muscle
and neural adaptation come back within weeks; connective tissue remodelling
takes months.

**So the first three weeks are deliberately, frustratingly easy.** Not a
formality. If you skip Phase 0 and go straight to heavy triples because 100kg
"used to be a warm-up", you will most likely be fine for about five weeks and
then something will go. Please don't.

I'm not a physio or a doctor. If anything sharp, one-sided, or joint-specific
turns up — particularly lower back on deadlifts or shoulder on bench — that's a
"stop and get it looked at" thing, not a "train around it" thing.

## Structure

**5 training days, 2 rest days.** Sessions 60–90 minutes.

| Day | Session | Primary |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bench Strength + Chest/Triceps | Bench press |
| 2 | Deadlift Strength + Back Thickness | Deadlift |
| 3 | **Rest** | — |
| 4 | Shoulders + Back Width + Arms | Overhead press |
| 5 | Legs + Core | Leg press / RDL |
| 6 | Upper Hypertrophy + Arms/Forearms | — |
| 7 | **Rest** | — |

**Back is trained on days 2, 4 and 6** — thickness, width, and hypertrophy
respectively. By design you should finish the week having hit lats, mid-back,
traps, rhomboids, rear delts and spinal erectors from multiple angles.

**Legs get one dedicated day, built around leg press, RDL and split squats
rather than heavy back squats** — per your preference. This is enough for
strength and athleticism. It is not enough to build elite quads, and that's the
trade you've chosen deliberately.

### One addition you didn't list

**Overhead press** isn't on your exercise list, but shoulders are a stated
priority and it's the single best strength driver for them — it also carries
over to bench lockout and shoulder stability. I've made it Day 4's primary
lift. If you dislike it, swap to seated dumbbell press and keep everything else;
just don't drop vertical pressing entirely or shoulders will lag behind
everything around them.

## Phase 0 — Reintroduction (Weeks 1–3)

**Goal:** technique, work capacity, connective tissue. Not load.

- **Every working set at RPE 5–6.** That means: finish every set feeling you
  could do 4–5 more reps. It should feel like you're undertraining. That's
  correct.
- **No sets to failure. None.** Not on isolation work either.
- Compounds: **3 × 8** at a weight you could do for 13+.
- Accessories: 2–3 × 12–15, same principle.
- If you're not sure what weight to use, go lighter. Week 1 is for finding out.
- Expect significant DOMS in weeks 1–2 even at these loads. Normal.

Run the same session layout as below, just with the loading above. At the end
of week 3, you'll do a top-set test to set your training maxes.

---

## Setting your Training Max (end of Week 3)

Don't test a true 1RM. Instead, on each main lift:

1. Ramp up in singles/doubles to a set of **3 reps at RPE 8** — meaning you're
   confident you had 2 more reps in the tank, no grinding, no form breakdown.
2. Estimated 1RM = that weight ÷ 0.90
3. **Training Max (TM) = estimated 1RM × 0.90**

Everything below is a percentage of **TM**, not of your true max. This is
deliberate — it builds in a buffer so you can progress for months without
constantly retesting.

Example: 3 reps at RPE 8 with 100kg → e1RM ≈ 111kg → **TM = 100kg**.

## Phase 1 — Base Building (Weeks 4–12)

### Main lift scheme (Bench, Deadlift, Overhead Press)

Each main lift gets a **top set** and **back-off sets**:

| Week in cycle | Top set | Back-offs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 × 5 @ 80% TM (RPE 7) | 3 × 5 @ 72% |
| 2 | 1 × 4 @ 85% TM (RPE 8) | 3 × 5 @ 75% |
| 3 | 1 × 3 @ 88% TM (RPE 8.5) | 3 × 5 @ 78% |
| 4 | **Deload** — 3 × 5 @ 60% | — |

Then repeat with **TM increased**:
- Upper body lifts (bench, OHP): **+2.5kg**
- Deadlift: **+5kg**

If you miss the top set target reps, don't increase TM that cycle — repeat it.
If you miss twice in a row, reduce TM by 10% and build back up. That's not a
setback, it's the system working.

### RPE reference

| RPE | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 10 | Nothing left, couldn't add weight or reps |
| 9 | 1 rep left |
| 8 | 2 reps left |
| 7 | 3 reps left |
| 5–6 | 4+ reps left — Phase 0 territory |

If a prescribed percentage feels heavier than the target RPE on the day, **go
by RPE and lower the weight**. Sleep, food and stress move your daily capacity
by 10%+ easily.

## The Sessions

### Warm-up (every session, ~8 minutes)

1. 5 min easy cardio — bike, rower, incline walk. Just raise temperature.
2. Dynamic work for the day's pattern:
   - **Press days:** band pull-aparts 2×15, shoulder dislocates, scap push-ups
   - **Pull/deadlift days:** cat-cow, hip hinges, dead hangs 2×20s
   - **Leg days:** leg swings, bodyweight split squats 2×10/side
3. Specific ramp on the first main lift — see below.

**Specific ramp for main lifts** (these don't count as working sets):
```
Empty bar × 10
40% × 5
55% × 4
70% × 2
80% × 1        (only on top-set weeks above 80%)
→ Top set
```

Rest periods: **3–5 min** after main-lift top and back-off sets. **2–3 min** on
secondary compounds. **60–90s** on isolation.

## Deloads

**Every 4th week**, as built into the cycle above. Main lifts drop to 3 × 5 @
60% TM; accessories cut to 2 sets each at normal weight.

Take an **unscheduled deload** if two or more of these show up:
- Top-set RPE climbing at the same weight for two sessions running
- Joints ache during warm-ups, not just after
- Sleep quality dropping without another cause
- Genuinely dreading training rather than just being tired

Deloading a week early costs nothing. Deloading a week late costs a month.

## Long-Term Progression (Year 1+)

1. **Weeks 1–3:** Phase 0 reintroduction
2. **Weeks 4–12:** Phase 1, three 4-week cycles, TM up each cycle
3. **Weeks 13–24:** Same structure — but if TM increases stop landing, reduce
   to +1.25kg upper / +2.5kg deadlift per cycle
4. **Month 6:** Retest TM properly. Expect to be at or near your old numbers.
5. **Beyond:** Rotate accessories every 8–12 weeks (swap T-bar for Pendlay rows,
   lat pulldown grips, incline angles). **Keep the main lifts fixed** — you
   progress those for years. Rotate the assistance, not the foundation.

The main lifts are bench, deadlift, overhead press. Those three numbers going up
over years *is* the programme. Everything else is support.

## Supplements

Ranked by strength of evidence, which is how you asked for it.

### Worth taking

| Supplement | Dose | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Creatine monohydrate** | 5g daily, any time, forever | The most well-supported ergogenic aid there is. No loading phase needed — 5g/day saturates in ~3–4 weeks. Don't buy fancier forms; monohydrate is the one that's been tested. |
| **Vitamin D3** | 1000–2000 IU daily, Oct–Mar minimum | Genuinely warranted in the UK. Deficiency is common here and affects strength, mood and immune function. Worth an actual blood test if your GP will do one. |
| **Whey protein** | As needed to hit target | Not magic — it's a convenient way to hit protein. If you hit your target with food, you don't need it. |
| **Caffeine** | 3–6mg/kg, 45min pre-training | Strong evidence for strength and endurance output. Cycle off periodically or tolerance builds. Not within 8h of sleep. |

### Reasonable, weaker evidence

| Supplement | Dose | Honest read |
|---|---|---|
| **Omega-3** | 1–2g combined EPA/DHA | Decent evidence for joint and cardiovascular health, weak for hypertrophy directly. Worth it if you don't eat oily fish twice a week. |
| **Magnesium glycinate** | 200–400mg, evening | Evidence for sleep is modest and mostly in deficient populations. Glycinate is the right form if you take it. Low risk, low-to-moderate reward. |

### The one you asked about specifically

**Ashwagandha** — the evidence is genuinely weaker than the others. There's
reasonable support for cortisol/stress reduction, and some small studies on
strength that are not well replicated. Two practical notes: there have been
case reports of liver issues, and it's the kind of supplement where quality
control varies a lot between brands. It's not in the "worth taking" list because
you asked me to avoid poorly-evidenced supplements and I'd be inconsistent to
include it. Not saying don't — saying know that it's a weaker bet than creatine.

### Skip

Anything marketed as a "test booster", BCAAs (redundant if protein is adequate),
glutamine, and most pre-workout blends beyond their caffeine content.

## Nutrition

### Protein

**1.8–2.2 g per kg bodyweight per day.** This is the one nutrition variable
genuinely worth being strict about. Spread across 3–5 meals, 30–50g per sitting.

### Calories

**I need your bodyweight, height and age to give you real numbers** — you
mentioned providing stats and haven't yet, so here's the method instead:

1. Maintenance ≈ **bodyweight (kg) × 33–35** for someone training 5×/week with
   an otherwise active job
2. Returning after a layoff with prior training history, you're in a good
   position to gain muscle while roughly maintaining weight — **start at
   maintenance**, not a surplus
3. Reassess after 4 weeks. Gaining >0.5kg/week means eat slightly less; strength
   stalling with no weight movement means eat slightly more

Send me your stats and I'll give you actual targets.

### Meal timing

Less important than total intake, with two exceptions worth bothering about:
- **Protein spread across the day** beats one huge hit
- **Something with carbs 1–2h before training** — you're training in the evening
  after work, so this matters more for you than most

### Hydration

3–4 litres a day, more on training days. Creatine increases intracellular water
demand, so this isn't optional once you're supplementing.

## Recovery

**Sleep is the highest-leverage recovery variable available to you**, ahead of
every supplement on the list above. 7–9 hours.

One factual note given you mentioned it: THC suppresses REM sleep, and REM is
part of what makes sleep restorative. Post-training rather than pre- is
sensible, and it's your call entirely — just worth knowing the mechanism, since
sleep quality is what your progression actually runs on.

**Your schedule is the real constraint here.** You're working Darams days and
GEH evenings 17:00–22:00 — that's a long day, and training has to fit around
it. Two practical consequences:

1. **Train on rest days from GEH where possible**, or before shifts rather than
   after. Training at 22:30 after a five-hour shift will wreck your sleep and
   your session quality.
2. **When the week goes badly, cut Day 6 first.** It's the accumulation day —
   losing it costs you the least. Never cut Day 2 (deadlift) or Day 1 (bench);
   those are the programme.

Once the Gym feature is built in Operator, the plan is for these sessions to
sit alongside your shifts on the Day Schedule, so you can see where training
actually fits each week rather than guessing.
